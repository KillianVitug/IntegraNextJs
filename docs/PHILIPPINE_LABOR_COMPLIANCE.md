# Philippine labor law and DOLE — standing project guidance

Adopted by the owner on **11 October 2026 (Asia/Manila)** for future app analysis and changes, including Integra attendance and payroll. Linked from [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) and [MASTER_PLAN.md](MASTER_PLAN.md).

This is a research and engineering safeguard, not a legal opinion or certification of the existing application. The sources below were consulted on 11 October 2026. Revalidate the affected provisions when making a new legal recommendation or implementing relevant behavior; a saved summary is not evidence that law or agency guidance remains unchanged.

## When and how to apply

1. Assess legal impact when scoping an analysis or change. Apply this guidance to work hours, schedules, punches, breaks, overtime, undertime, lateness, leave, wages, deductions, benefits and approval workflows, including their UI explanations, rounding, defaults, imports, corrections, generated DTR and provisional/official payroll calculations. A purely cosmetic or infrastructure change with no such effect needs only that scope assessment.
2. Establish the applicable employee coverage, work date, region, day type, employment terms, company policy/CBA and any special work arrangement. Do not assume every employee has the same statutory coverage, that a job title proves an exemption, or that every scheduled hour is compensable.
3. Use primary sources: applicable law and implementing rules, current DOLE issuances/handbooks, NWPC/regional wage orders and Supreme Court decisions. For other statutory obligations use the responsible authority, such as BIR, SSS, PhilHealth or Pag-IBIG. Search results, pending public questions, blogs, bills and prior AI explanations are not binding rules. Distinguish an enacted rule from a proposal and a fact-specific judgment from a universal rule.
4. State separately what the law requires, what a more favorable contract/CBA/company benefit provides, what the owner has adopted, and what the app actually does. Record the source URL, provision, date checked, relevant effective date, applicability and uncertainty. Do not use an earlier engineering decision or successful test as legal approval.
5. Identify a suspected conflict explicitly and propose the smallest supported correction. Do not silently reduce an existing benefit, invent a deduction or change historical payroll. Where a material interpretation or missing business fact prevents a sound pay-affecting decision, identify the precise issue for DOLE or Philippine labor counsel; continue unaffected work. This is not a requirement to seek legal approval for every task.
6. Verify the relevant calculation and failure cases with isolated data when code changes: compensable time, day type, breaks, actual versus rounded minutes, approval/evidence, lawful unpaid time, benefit preservation and downstream payroll effects as applicable. Keep original captures, audit history and existing payroll recovery/closed-run safeguards. Record verified behavior and remaining legal questions in task evidence and the checkpoint.

## Starting references and boundaries

The following are a bounded starting point from the overtime discussion, not a complete audit of wages, leave, benefits or all Philippine labor law.

| Topic | Working reference and limits |
|---|---|
| Normal hours and overtime | For covered employees under the ordinary regime, statutory overtime generally concerns compensable work beyond eight hours in a day. Each ordinary-day OT hour earns at least 125% of the regular hourly rate; each rest-day/holiday OT hour earns at least 130% of the hourly rate applicable to the first eight hours of that rest day/holiday. Verify special arrangements and more favorable terms. See [Labor Code Articles 82–87](https://blr.dole.gov.ph/2014/12/11/book-iii-conditions-of-employment/) and the [DOLE 2024 Handbook, Overtime Pay](https://nwpc.dole.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Workers-Statutory-Monetary-Benefits-Handbook-2024-Edition.pdf); check for later applicable issuances. |
| Compensable time and breaks | Required duty and work suffered/permitted can count, including qualifying waiting time and short rest breaks. Productive activity is not the sole test. An unpaid meal assumption requires the actual arrangement to support it; do not deduct the same break twice. See [Omnibus Rules, Book III, Rule I, sections 3–7](https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1974/pd_442_1974b.html). |
| Short overtime and rounding | The general Article 87/Rule I section 8 text sets no blanket 30- or 60-minute qualifying minimum. Do not treat an app's configured minimum or legacy rounding as a statutory rule, or silently discard otherwise compensable time. Determine coverage, evidence and the applicable arrangement before concluding that any particular minutes are owed. See the [Code](https://blr.dole.gov.ph/2014/12/11/book-iii-conditions-of-employment/) and [implementing rules](https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1974/pd_442_1974b.html). |
| Authorization and proof | A late clock-out alone does not establish overtime work, and a missing app approval alone does not settle entitlement. Check required/permitted work, employer or supervisor knowledge and the applicable authorization policy. [Robina Farms v. Villa, G.R. 175869, 18 April 2016](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/apr2016/gr_175869_2016.html) rejected a claim based on extended presence without sufficient proof and applied the authorization requirement; it also discusses the implementing-rule knowledge test. |
| Undertime and lateness | Distinguish pay for time genuinely not worked from an additional monetary penalty. Paid leave, wage basis and more favorable terms can affect treatment. Do not charge the same shortfall twice or forfeit earned pay through unsupported rounding/penalties. The general no-work/no-pay principle is discussed with contractual benefit limits in [COPPER v. PASAR, G.R. 270699, 21 April 2026](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/18/70547); that case is not a specific approval of this app's tardiness rules. |
| Offsetting | [Article 88](https://blr.dole.gov.ph/2014/12/11/book-iii-conditions-of-employment/) expressly prohibits offsetting one day's undertime with another day's overtime. It does not alone settle same-day lateness/extension. Examine compensable hours, authorized arrangements, existing benefits and any leave/pay deduction. [NWSA v. NWSA Consolidated Unions, G.R. L-18939, 31 August 1964](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1964/aug1964/gr_l-18939_1964.html) addresses unfair treatment where undertime was both charged to leave and offset against overtime; do not generalize that factual context without checking applicability. |

## Scenario: truck driver returns after shift because of traffic

Source review: **11 October 2026**. This is general scenario analysis, not an employee coverage/pay determination or approved app change.

Applying [Omnibus Rules, Book III, Rule I, sections 3–5 and 8](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1989/omnibus_labor_1989.html), a driver still completing an assigned delivery or required depot return generally remains on duty during traffic. Compensable hours and applicable coverage determine overtime; the scheduled end alone does not end duty. Assess actual off-duty breaks and personal travel separately.

Drivers are not automatically exempt field personnel. [Far East Agricultural Supply v. Lebatique, G.R. 162813, 12 February 2007](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2007/feb2007/gr_162813_2007.html) found a truck driver covered where delivery instructions and required waiting during truck bans demonstrated employer supervision/control. Assess whether work hours can be determined with reasonable certainty; do not infer exemption from title or workplace location alone.

Proposed app treatment: preserve the scheduled end and actual duty completion; use dispatch/delivery records and supervisor evidence to verify a reason such as traffic or delivery delay. Keep pending review explicit and resolve entitlement promptly for payroll. A missing separate OT checkbox must not alone determine unpaid time for a continuing assigned trip. Keep attendance/payment review separate from any investigation of avoidable delay. These workflow recommendations are not implemented by this note.

## Implications for the current application

- The [11 October after-shift label analysis](../artifacts/after-shift-ot-label-analysis-20261011.txt) established current software behavior only. It did not establish legal entitlement or approve zero overtime payment for the inspected workday. Keep personal attendance and pay details out of these general notes.
- Keep captured time, compensable time, time after scheduled end, detected OT, authorized/approved OT and paid OT distinguishable. A label or review filter must not turn an unverified payroll assumption into a legal conclusion.
- Current eight-hour calculations, configured eligibility thresholds, minute rounding, approval gates and accumulated-lateness penalties remain implemented/adopted project facts where recorded in the master plan. They are not certified legally compliant by this guidance. If a task relies on one of them, assess its legal basis and applicable benefits rather than assuming it is settled law.
- Exact same-day treatment, employee coverage and factual authorization/meal arrangements can remain unresolved. Do not infer them from a punch pair alone or silently revise payroll to resolve uncertainty.
